## 1. Rue Montorgueil

**Verified/Corrections**
- ✅ Stohrer, 51 rue Montorgueil, founded 1730 by Nicolas Stohrer, pastry chef of Marie Leszczyńska (wife of Louis XV) – oldest pâtisserie in Paris. https://archik.fr/en/quartiers/montorgueil/
- ✅ Painted decor: walls and ceiling painted in 1864 by Paul Baudry – the painter of the grand foyer of the Opéra Garnier. (The parents' "painted facade" is really the painted interior + the glass panels; say "painted shop".) https://archik.fr/en/quartiers/montorgueil/
- ❔ Baba au rhum origin: the shop's own legend (invented for King Stanislas Leszczyński, Marie's father, from a dry brioche soaked in wine, later rum). Legend, not documented fact – present it as "the story Stohrer tells".
- ❌ Au Rocher de Cancale "no. 78, 1804 facade": the 1804 restaurant (Alexis Balaine; Balzac's characters dine there) was at **no. 59**; it went bankrupt in 1846 and the name later re-opened at **no. 78**, the present house, whose first-floor frescoes are attributed to the caricaturist Gavarni. So: 1804 = the name; the building/decor at no. 78 = mid-19th c. https://marais-louvre.fr/petite-histoire-du-restaurant-au-rocher-de-cancale ; https://paris-promeneurs.com/restaurant-au-rocher-de-cancale/
- ❔ Passage du Grand-Cerf 1825: date widely given (some say 1835); highest glass roof of the Paris passages (~11.8 m). Not re-verified.
- ❔ Street-numbering decree: 4 Feb 1805 (15 pluviôse an XIII): odd numbers left / even right, walking away from the Seine; on streets parallel to the river numbers rise with the current (upstream→downstream). Standard fact, not re-verified.

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. The pastry shop is painted by the same man who painted the Opéra's foyer: Paul Baudry (1864). It is the cheapest "Opéra Garnier ceiling" ticket in Paris – the price of an éclair. https://archik.fr/en/quartiers/montorgueil/
2. Balzac used the (first) Rocher de Cancale as a set: his fictional dandies from La Comédie humaine dine there. A restaurant that exists in a novel and on the street at once. https://marais-louvre.fr/petite-histoire-du-restaurant-au-rocher-de-cancale
3. Paris house numbers are a compass: the Seine is the zero line, and numbers grow as you walk away from the river. In 1805 Napoleon's prefect fixed the rule that still runs the whole city. ❔ (standard fact)

**Looking exercise**
"Where is the river?" – without a map, use only door numbers on two streets to deduce the direction of the Seine, then check. Then a 60-second **blind-contour drawing** of the nymph on Stohrer's painted glass (eyes on the panel, never on the paper).

## 2. Angelina, 226 rue de Rivoli

**Verified/Corrections**
- ❔ 1903 founding by Austrian confectioner Antoine Rumpelmayer, named after his daughter-in-law; "L'Africain" hot chocolate; Mont-Blanc; Proust and Coco Chanel as regulars – all stated on Angelina's own site (angelina-paris.fr); not re-fetched this session. No contradiction found.

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. ❔ The Belle Époque interior is by Édouard-Jean Niermans, the architect of the Moulin Rouge and Folies Bergère – so the tearoom is designed by a cabaret architect. (angelina-paris.fr, general knowledge)
2. ❔ The Mont-Blanc is a 1903 sculpture problem: a meringue base, a dome of whipped cream, then chestnut "vermicelli" piped to look like a mountain – a food that is literally a landscape model.
3. ❔ Proust reportedly took his chocolate here; the tearoom is 200 m from the Tuileries where he walked as a child in *Du côté de chez Swann*.

**Looking exercise**
Draw the Mont-Blanc as a **cross-section** (geologist's cut) before eating it; then match each layer to a paint colour (name the exact shade: "raw umber", "ivory"...). The 3-year-old counts the "spaghetti" strands.

## 3. Palais-Royal

**Verified/Corrections**
- ✅ Daniel Buren, *Les Deux Plateaux* (1985–86): 260 black-and-white striped marble columns over ~3,000 m² of the Cour d'Honneur; stripes 8.7 cm wide (Buren's fixed signature width). https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonnes_de_Buren ; https://passerelles.essentiels.bnf.fr/fr/image/204fb75e-95fe-46b3-85f0-60e53f71245d-colonnes-buren-palais-royal
- ❔ Camille Desmoulins, 12 July 1789, jumps on a table at the Café de Foy and calls Paris to arms, plucking a leaf as a green cockade – standard history, not re-verified.

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. The title means "two levels": one grid of columns stands in the courtyard, a second grid is buried – you see their tops through grates, with water flowing over them. The work you walk on is bigger than the one you see. https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonnes_de_Buren
2. Every stripe Buren has made since 1965 is 8.7 cm wide – the width of the awning fabric he bought at a Paris market. Same width here as on his works worldwide. https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonnes_de_Buren
3. In 1986 Parisians hated it (petitions, court cases); in 2010 the State restored it for ~€5m. Same object, opposite verdicts 25 years apart. https://www.parisladouce.com/2018/11/paris-les-deux-plateaux-grandeur-et.html

**Looking exercise**
Photograph one column **from ground level** so it looks as tall as the palace behind it (forced perspective). Then measure a stripe with a phone ruler app – does it really say 8.7 cm? Bonus: look down a grate and count buried columns.

## 4. Tuileries

**Verified/Corrections**
- ❔ Tuileries Palace burned May 1871 (Commune), ruins demolished 1883, never rebuilt – standard history.
- ❌ (nuance) "The axis is not straight because of the fire": the bend (~6°) between the Louvre's axis and the Champs-Élysées axis exists because the Tuileries Palace (1564) was built parallel to the river, on a different alignment from the Louvre's Cour Carrée. The fire did not create the kink; the demolition only made it visible – today the Arc du Carrousel sits on the historical axis while the Pyramid sits on the Louvre's axis. (General knowledge; not re-verified.)

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. ❔ From the Arc du Carrousel you can line up the Obelisk, the Arc de Triomphe and the Grande Arche 8 km away – but turn round and the Pyramid is off to the side. The "perfect" axis has a kink built in since the 1500s.
2. ❔ The garden is an open-air sculpture museum: 20th-c. works by Giacometti, Louise Bourgeois, Dubuffet, Maillol sit among the 17th-c. layout by Le Nôtre (Versailles' gardener).
3. ❔ The word "Tuileries" = tile factories: the palace was built on a site of tile kilns.

**Looking exercise**
Stand at the Arc du Carrousel, sight the Obelisk–Arc de Triomphe line with a thumb, then rotate to the Pyramid and photograph "the kink" (one photo each way). Count how many sculptures you can find that are NOT of a person.

## 6. 59 Rivoli

**Verified/Corrections**
- ✅ Open Tue–Sun 13:00–20:00, closed Monday; free entry ("entrée libre gratuite"); 30 artists in residence; occupied 1999, bought by the City of Paris 2001. https://59rivoli.org/
- ✅ Concerts every Sat and Sun at 18:00, Sept–June, free. https://www.sortiraparis.com/en/what-to-visit-in-paris/exhibit-museum/articles/218397-59-rivoli-the-free-artist-squat-on-rue-de-rivoli-in-paris

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. It began as an illegal squat in an abandoned bank building in Nov 1999; instead of evicting it, the City bought the building (2001) and made it legal – a squat that won. https://59rivoli.org/
2. About half the 30 studios rotate every few months, so the building you see is never the same twice; the facade itself is a changing installation. https://59rivoli.org/
3. It calls itself an "aftersquat" – the third most visited contemporary-art venue in Paris by its own count, with zero ticket price. https://www.sortiraparis.com/en/what-to-visit-in-paris/exhibit-museum/articles/218397-59-rivoli-the-free-artist-squat-on-rue-de-rivoli-in-paris

**Looking exercise**
On each of the 6 floors pick ONE studio and write one material you have never seen used for art. Ask one artist a single question ("what did you throw away this week?"). Photograph the stairwell straight up from the ground floor.

## 7. Marais

**Verified/Corrections**
- ✅ Hôtel de Sens: cannonball (orange-sized) embedded in the facade with "28 juillet 1830" carved beneath, said to date from the Trois Glorieuses. Nuance: historians doubt it – the ball and inscription look "carefully arranged". Marguerite de Valois lived there 1605–06. https://breves-histoire.fr/vestiges/boulet-canon-hotel-sens/ ; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_des_archev%C3%AAques_de_Sens
- ❔ Place des Vosges inaugurated 1612 (Louis XIII/Anne of Austria engagement), 36 pavilions (9 per side); Victor Hugo at no. 6, 1832–48; Maison de Victor Hugo permanent collection free (Paris Musées) – standard, not re-verified.
- ❔ Guimard's Agoudas Hakehilos synagogue, 10 rue Pavée, 1913 – his only religious building. Bombing: the Paris synagogues were dynamited on the night of **2–3 Oct 1941**; Yom Kippur 1941 fell on 1 Oct, so "bombed on Yom Kippur" is approximate – say "two nights after Yom Kippur 1941". (Dates from general knowledge – verify.)
- ❔ Vel d'Hiv roundup 16–17 July 1942: 13,152 arrested (incl. ~4,115 children) – official figure, standard.
- ❔ L'As du Fallafel's Lenny Kravitz quote: painted on the shop's sign; origin of the quote not verified.
- ❔ Sacha Finkelsztajn ("La Boutique Jaune", 27 rue des Rosiers) since 1946 – standard.
- ✅ Jardin des Rosiers – Joseph-Migneret: created in stages 2007–2014, third section opened 5 July 2014 and named for Joseph Migneret (headmaster 1920–44, Righteous Among the Nations). Contains a remnant of one of the 77 towers of the Philippe Auguste wall (13th c.). https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jardin_des_Rosiers_-_Joseph-Migneret ; https://www.paris.fr/lieux/jardin-des-rosiers-joseph-migneret-2828
- ✅ School plaque, École des Hospitalières-Saint-Gervais: 165 Jewish pupils deported and murdered; plaque placed 7 May 1971. https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_%C3%A9l%C3%A9mentaire_des_Hospitali%C3%A8res-Saint-Gervais
- ❔ Marché des Enfants Rouges 1615, oldest covered market in Paris – standard.
- ✅/❔ Musée Picasso Paris: closed Monday; Tue–Sun 9:30–18:00 (Sortiraparis, updated 9 Apr 2026); €16 full, €12 reduced; under-18 free is standard for national museums but was not confirmed (official site blocked the fetch). https://www.sortiraparis.com/en/what-to-visit-in-paris/exhibit-museum/articles/211538-musee-picasso-in-paris-prices-access-opening-hours-everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-parisian-museum ; official: https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. The cannonball may be a fake – a 19th-c. "installation" of a revolution. Detective question: why would a ball fired in street fighting land so neatly, with a carved caption? https://breves-histoire.fr/vestiges/boulet-canon-hotel-sens/
2. ❔ Place des Vosges is partly trompe-l'œil: some "brick" facades are plaster painted to imitate brick, because real brick ran over budget in 1605–12. Find the seams.
3. The little Marais garden hides a piece of the 13th-c. city wall of Philippe Auguste (one of its 77 towers) – Paris' medieval border is now in a playground. https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jardin_des_Rosiers_-_Joseph-Migneret
4. Guimard, the Métro-entrance designer, built exactly one religious building in his life: this synagogue, whose narrow facade undulates like a curtain. ❔

**Looking exercise**
Place des Vosges: **spot the fake brick** – find a facade where the "bricks" are painted plaster (peeling patches give it away) and one with real brick; photograph both side by side. At the Hôtel de Sens, find the cannonball and vote: real or staged? At Migneret garden, put a hand on the medieval wall and estimate its thickness.

## 8. Eiffel Tower

**Verified/Corrections**
- ✅ 674 steps from the ground to the 2nd floor (stairs stop there; lift only to the top). https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/faq/spot/how-can-you-walk-eiffel-tower-foot
- ✅ Repainted about every 7 years (Eiffel's own recommendation), 60 tons of paint. https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/the-monument/painting-eiffel-tower
- ✅ 18,038 metal parts, 2.5 million rivets. https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/the-monument/history
- ✅ Thermal movement: on a sunny day the top traces a curve ~15 cm across (and the tower grows a few cm in heat). https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/news/history-and-culture/why-does-eiffel-tower-change-size
- ❔ "Eiffel Tower brown" in 3 shades (darker at the base, lighter at the top to counter the sky's haze) – on the official painting page; not quoted in the fetched excerpt.
- ❔ Maupassant lunch anecdote (ate in the tower restaurant "because it is the only place I can't see it") – legend, standard.
- ❔ Saved in 1909 by radio/telegraphy uses when the 20-year concession ended – standard.
- ❔ Designers: Maurice Koechlin & Émile Nouguier (engineers) drew the first sketch in 1884; Stephen Sauvestre added the arches and decoration; Eiffel bought the patent – official history page. https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/the-monument/history
- ✅ Top floor: closed 5 Jan–6 Feb 2026 for annual maintenance, reopened 7 Feb 2026. No closure announced for Sept 2026. One-day staff strike on 7 Sept 2026 (reopened 8 Sept). https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/news/visit/everything-know-about-closing-top-january ; https://www.franceinfo.fr/culture/patrimoine/tour-eiffel-le-personnel-en-greve-apres-une-visite-privee-polemique_8181671.html

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. The tower is painted in three shades of the same brown, darkest at the bottom – a painter's trick (atmospheric perspective) so it looks one colour against the sky. https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/the-monument/painting-eiffel-tower
2. The top moves: over a sunny day the summit sways in a ~15 cm circle, leaning away from the sun as the metal on the sunny side expands. https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/news/history-and-culture/why-does-eiffel-tower-change-size
3. Eiffel didn't design it – two of his engineers did, and an architect was brought in to make it "pretty"; Eiffel bought the rights and his name stuck. https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/the-monument/history
4. ❔ 72 scientists' names are painted in gold around the first floor (Foucault, Ampère, Lavoisier...); no women were included – a fact worth the girls noticing.

**Looking exercise**
From Trocadéro: hold a pencil at arm's length and record the tower's height in "pencil units" at three distances. Up close: photograph the base colour and the top colour against the same sky and compare in the phone. Find three of the 72 names you recognise.

## 9. Sainte-Chapelle

**Verified/Corrections**
- ✅ 1,113 scenes/panels in 15 bays; windows 15 m high in the nave, 13 m in the choir; about two-thirds of the glass is original 13th-c. https://www.sainte-chapelle.fr/en/discover/history-of-the-sainte-chapelle ; https://www.sainte-chapelle.fr/en/discover/a-unique-set-of-stained-glass-windows
- ✅ Built in under 7 years (consecrated 1248) by Louis IX for the Crown of Thorns, bought 1239 from Baldwin II of Courtenay, Latin emperor of Constantinople, for 135,000 livres tournois – "half the annual income of the kingdom". https://www.sainte-chapelle.fr/en/discover/history-of-the-sainte-chapelle
- ✅ Revolution: used as a flour warehouse, then archive depository 1803–1838; restored 1840–63 (Duban, Lassus, Boeswillwald); spire (75 m) is the fifth. same source
- ✅ Crown of Thorns survived the 15 April 2019 fire; treasury objects were carried out by a human chain of ~100 civil servants, police and municipal workers. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Paris_fire
- ❔ "Rescued by the chaplain": Fr Jean-Marc Fournier, chaplain of the Paris fire brigade, is widely credited (news reports April 2019) with entering with firefighters to retrieve the Crown and the Blessed Sacrament. Not confirmed from an official source this session; the relic was kept in the Louvre and returned to Notre-Dame in Dec 2024 (reports).
- ❔ Hours Sept 2026: high season normally 9:00–19:00 daily (CMN); not fetched – check sainte-chapelle.fr.

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. The relic cost 135,000 livres – half the kingdom's yearly income – and the whole chapel built to hold it cost far less. A box worth more than the building. https://www.sainte-chapelle.fr/en/discover/history-of-the-sainte-chapelle
2. The glaziers had only ~7 colours: red, blue, two greens, two purples, a yellow, plus clear glass. Everything else is painted onto the glass. https://www.sainte-chapelle.fr/en/discover/a-unique-set-of-stained-glass-windows
3. The 1,113 scenes run like a comic: start north with Genesis, end west with the Apocalypse rose – yet the monument itself says the windows "are not meant to be read but to be seen". same source
4. After 1789 the royal chapel stored flour. Sacks of flour under 15-m windows. https://www.sainte-chapelle.fr/en/discover/history-of-the-sainte-chapelle

**Looking exercise**
Choose one bay and estimate the percentage of red vs blue (write a guess, then count 20 panels). Photograph the coloured light on the floor and on someone's hand. Find the bay that tells the story of the Crown itself (Louis IX carrying the relic – the "relics bay").

## 10. Île de la Cité

**Verified/Corrections**
- ❔ Conciergerie clock, Tour de l'Horloge: first public clock in Paris, installed 1370–71 (Henri de Vic), gilded/blue restoration 2012 – standard (CMN).
- ❔ Marie Antoinette: 2 Aug–16 Oct 1793 = 76 days – standard.
- ❔ ~2,700 (usually ~2,780) condemned by the Revolutionary Tribunal and sent from here to the guillotine – standard.
- ❔ Pont Neuf: completed 1607, first Paris bridge without houses and with pavements – standard.
- ❌ Henri IV statue: recast 1818 (Lemot). In 2004 restorers found seven sealed containers (4 boxes in the horse, 3 small cylinders in the head) – the 3 secret ones were slipped in by the chaser Balthazar Mesnel, a Bonapartist, and held anti-Bourbon writings. **No Napoleon statuette was found** – that part is legend. https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/histoire-insolite-paris/statue-henri-iv-pont-neuf-tresor/ ; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_%C3%A9questre_d'Henri_IV
- ❔ Place Dauphine (1607–16), triangular, named for the Dauphin (future Louis XIII) – standard.

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. The king's horse is a time capsule: in 2004 restorers opened boxes sealed in 1818 – including secret ones hidden by a Napoleon fan on the team. https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/histoire-insolite-paris/statue-henri-iv-pont-neuf-tresor/
2. ❔ The bronze for Henri IV came partly from melting down Napoleon's statue from the Vendôme column – a royalist statue made of an emperor. (Widely stated; not fetched.)
3. ❔ The Pont Neuf carries 381 carved faces (mascarons) along its cornices – no two alike.

**Looking exercise**
Mascaron hunt: walk one side of the Pont Neuf and photograph the five strangest faces; each girl sketches one in 30 seconds. Count arches: how many on the Left-Bank side vs the Right-Bank side (they differ).

## 11. Notre-Dame

**Verified/Corrections**
- ✅ Reopened 7 Dec 2024 (ceremony), public from 8 Dec 2024. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Paris_fire
- ❔ ~2,000 oaks for the new roof and spire – official Rebâtir figure, not fetched.
- ✅/❔ Free entry; free timed slots via notredamedeparis.fr or the app "Cathédrale Notre-Dame de Paris". Third-party guides (2026) say slots open **about 2 days ahead, around midnight Paris time**, plus same-day releases; walk-in without slot possible but with long queues in high season. Official page blocked the fetch – verify. https://paris-notredame.com/reservation-system/ ; https://thebettervacation.com/paris/notre-dame-paris/
- ❔ Point Zéro: bronze star in the parvis (1924), origin of French road distances – standard.
- ❌ Emmanuel bell (13 t, clapper 500 kg) rang for the Liberation on **24 Aug 1944 around 21:00** (Musée Carnavalet photo caption), not 25 Aug. https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/liberation-de-paris-24-aout-1944-on-fait-sonner-la-cloche-du-gros-bourdon ; https://www.tours-notre-dame-de-paris.fr/en/discover/notre-dame-and-the-liberation-of-paris

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. Emmanuel was baptised by Louis XIV himself as godfather; its clapper alone weighs 500 kg; 8 police officers rang it by hand on 24 Aug 1944 while the fighting was still on. https://www.tours-notre-dame-de-paris.fr/en/discover/notre-dame-and-the-liberation-of-paris
2. The relics survived because they were in the sacristy the fire never reached; a human chain of ~100 people passed the treasury out. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Paris_fire
3. ❔ The new golden rooster on the spire (wings like flames) holds relics and a sealed list of the ~2,000 people who rebuilt the cathedral – their names are 96 m up.

**Looking exercise**
Stand on the Point Zéro star for a "0 km" photo. Then a **stone-colour test**: photograph new blond limestone next to old grey stone and estimate how many years until they match. Count the chimeras you can see from the parvis without zoom.

## 14. Jardin du Luxembourg

**Verified/Corrections**
- ✅ Carousel built 1879 from drawings by Charles Garnier (architect of the Opéra); still has the old ring game (jeu de bagues). https://jardin.senat.fr/en-famille/le-manege.html ; https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/lieux-insolites/le-manege-du-luxembourg-lautre-oeuvre-de-charles-garnier/
- ❌ "106 statues": the Senate's own page is titled "Les 102 statues du Jardin du Luxembourg"; 106 appears in secondary sources. Use "about a hundred (the Senate counts 102)". https://jardin.senat.fr/histoire-patrimoine/patrimoine/les-statues-du-jardin.html
- ❔ Statue of Liberty: Bartholdi bronze (1900) moved to the Musée d'Orsay in 2012, replaced by a copy – standard.
- ✅ Toy sailboats: "Les Voiliers du Luxembourg" – handmade wooden boats since the 19th c., 30-min hire, steered with a wooden stick; ❔ Sept weekday operation not confirmed (typically weekends + fine weather). https://www.sortiraparis.com/en/what-to-visit-in-paris/walks/articles/317791-les-voiliers-du-luxembourg-les-petits-bateaux-historiques-du-jardin-du-luxembourg-for-children

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. The carousel horses were drawn by the man who designed the Paris Opéra – the cheapest Garnier in town (a couple of euros a ride). https://jardin.senat.fr/en-famille/le-manege.html
2. ❔ The 20 statues ringing the central terrace are all women – the "Queens of France and famous women" ordered by Louis-Philippe in the 1840s.
3. ❔ The Medici fountain's basin is a perspective trick: the water looks like it slopes toward you.

**Looking exercise**
Pick one queen statue and draw **only her hands**. Count the queens (should be 20). At the carousel, count how many rings a child catches in one ride.

## 15. Seine cruise

**Verified/Corrections**
- ❔ Eiffel sparkle: first lit 31 Dec 1999 for the millennium, permanent since 2003; 5 minutes at the start of every hour after dark, currently until 23:45 (since 2022) – official (toureiffel.paris), not fetched.
- ❔ 37 bridges over the Seine within Paris – standard (Paris.fr).
- ❔ Pont Alexandre III inaugurated 1900 (Exposition Universelle), foundation stone laid by Tsar Nicholas II in 1896; single steel arch only ~6 m high so as not to block the view of the Invalides – standard.

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. ❔ The sparkle (20,000 bulbs) is a protected artwork: the tower by day is public domain, but its night lighting is copyrighted – a monument with an evening licence.
2. ❔ The Zouave soldier statue on the Pont de l'Alma is Paris' unofficial flood gauge – when the water reaches his feet, the riverside roads close.
3. ❔ The Pont des Arts (1804) was Paris' first iron bridge.

**Looking exercise**
Count bridges as you pass and note one detail per bridge (face, lamp, statue). Watch for the Zouave's feet. Photograph the sparkle with a **steady 5-second phone exposure** (rest the phone on the rail).

## 16. Montmartre

**Verified/Corrections**
- ✅ Sacré-Cœur: exterior stone from Château-Landon (Seine-et-Marne), a limestone/travertine that exudes calcite in rain and whitens itself. https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilique_du_Sacr%C3%A9-C%C5%93ur_de_Montmartre
- ❔ Built 1875–1914, consecrated 1919 – standard.
- ❌ "83 pillars 40 m deep": 83 pillars/wells, **33 m deep**, through marl and sand into the gypsum. https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilique_du_Sacr%C3%A9-C%C5%93ur_de_Montmartre
- ✅ La Savoyarde bell: cast 1895 in Annecy, ~19 t (18,835 kg), hauled to Paris by 28 horses, blessed 21 Nov 1895, hoisted into the campanile 1907. same source
- ❔ Funicular ~90 s, one t+ ticket – standard (RATP).
- ❌ "~300 artists, 1 m² each": the Carré aux artistes has **124 numbered spots** (67 painters, 45 portraitists, 7 caricaturists, etc.), allocated by the City; the "300 artists" figure is the number of permit-holders sharing the spots in half-day shifts – not confirmed. https://cdn.paris.fr/paris/2024/02/22/aap-tertre-2024-3fjY.pdf ; https://www.montmartre-connect.com/blog/place-du-tertre-coeur-artistique-montmartre/
- ✅ Clos Montmartre: the City counts 1,762 vines (~27 grape varieties); roughly 1,500–1,800 half-bottles/bottles a year, sold at auction for the 18th arrondissement's social works. https://www.paris.fr/lieux/clos-montmartre-2697 ; https://www.montmartre-connect.com/blog/vignes-montmartre-histoire-et-secrets/
- ❔ Saint-Pierre de Montmartre consecrated 1147 – standard.
- ❔ Le Passe-Muraille bronze (1989) by actor-sculptor Jean Marais after Marcel Aymé's 1943 story – standard.
- ❔ Bateau-Lavoir: Picasso paints Les Demoiselles d'Avignon there 1907; burned 12 May 1970; rebuilt 1978 (studios still in use) – standard.
- ❔ Café des Deux Moulins (Amélie, 2001); Van Gogh at 54 rue Lepic with brother Theo, 1886–88 – standard.
- ❔ Le Mur des je t'aime (2000, Frédéric Baron & calligrapher Claire Kito): 612 enamelled lava tiles, 311 "I love you", 250 languages – official figures (lesjetaime.com), not fetched.
- ❔ Abbesses: deepest station (~36 m); Guimard glass canopy moved here from Hôtel de Ville in 1974 – standard.

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. The basilica cleans itself: Château-Landon stone sweats calcite when it rains, so the white gets whiter with age instead of blacker. https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilique_du_Sacr%C3%A9-C%C5%93ur_de_Montmartre
2. The hill is hollow (old gypsum quarries – "plaster of Paris"), so the church stands on 83 concrete stilts 33 m deep; without them it would sink. same source
3. A 19-ton bell arrived by road in 1895 behind 28 horses – then waited 12 years to be hoisted. same source
4. ❔ The Mur des je t'aime's red splashes are pieces of a broken heart; assembled, they would make one whole heart – a wall about mending.
5. ❔ Saint-Pierre's four marble columns are Roman, recycled from an ancient temple that stood on the hill – Montmartre = "Mons Martis"/"Mons Martyrum".

**Looking exercise**
Mur des je t'aime: find the Hebrew tile (אני אוהב אותך) and count how many scripts you can identify in 2 minutes. Place du Tertre: watch one portraitist for 3 minutes and note the **first three lines** they draw (where does a face start?). Sacré-Cœur: find a grey patch and a white patch and explain the difference with the rain story.

## 17. Eurostar / Channel Tunnel

**Verified/Corrections**
- ✅ Service-tunnel breakthrough 1 Dec 1990: Philippe Cozette (FR) and Graham Fagg (UK) shook hands; alignment error at the meeting point **358 mm horizontal, 58 mm vertical** after ~38 km of boring. https://www.francebleu.fr/infos/societe/tunnel-sous-la-manche-philippe-cozette-l-homme-qui-relie-l-angleterre-au-continent-1399348800 ; https://www.lemoniteur.fr/article/il-y-a-20-ans-s-achevait-le-percement-du-tunnel-sous-la-manche.1289839
- ❔ 50.45 km long, 37.9 km under the sea (longest undersea section in the world), up to 75 m below the seabed; trains take ~20–35 min in the tunnel at max 160 km/h; 300 km/h only on the high-speed lines outside – standard (Eurostar/Getlink), not re-fetched.
- ❔ 1802: mining engineer Albert Mathieu-Favier proposes a tunnel for horse-drawn coaches lit by oil lamps, with an artificial island mid-Channel to change horses – standard.
- ❔ 1880–82: Beaumont & English boring machines dig ~1.9 km from Shakespeare Cliff (Dover) and ~1.7 km from Sangatte; abandoned 1882–83 after British military objections (invasion fear) – standard.

**Surprising facts for teens**
1. Two teams dug toward each other for 38 km and missed by 36 cm sideways and 6 cm up-down. https://www.lemoniteur.fr/article/il-y-a-20-ans-s-achevait-le-percement-du-tunnel-sous-la-manche.1289839
2. The two men who shook hands through the chalk were chosen by lottery among the workers. https://www.francebleu.fr/infos/societe/tunnel-sous-la-manche-philippe-cozette-l-homme-qui-relie-l-angleterre-au-continent-1399348800
3. ❔ The 1802 plan had a man-made island halfway across to swap tired horses; the 1880 Victorian tunnels still exist under the cliffs.

**Looking exercise**
Stopwatch the tunnel (dark to light) and compute average speed from 50.45 km (maths for the 14-year-old, drawing for the 13-year-old: sketch the three-tube cross-section). Feel your ears at the entry – pressure is real physics, not imagination.
