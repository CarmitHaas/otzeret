### A3. "Proper art" angle — Le Château de la Belle au Bois Dormant
- ✅ Designer: Imagineer **Tom Morris**. Height **50 m**. Completed for 12 April 1992. (Wikipedia)
- ✅ Inspirations: **Les Très Riches Heures du duc de Berry** (illuminated book of hours, c. 1412–16, Musée Condé, Chantilly) and **Mont-Saint-Michel**. (Wikipedia; Disney Wiki) ❔ Also cited by a single fan source (disneyinsights): Sainte-Chapelle windows, Saint-Séverin's twisted pillars, Chambord's dome, Hospices de Beaune tiled roofs, 16 turrets, 14 shades of pink, gold snails on the turrets.
- ✅ **Why more fairy-tale than Anaheim**: real European castles are nearby, so Imagineers deliberately chose a stylised storybook castle instead of a "realistic" one. Pink walls + royal-blue roofs. (Wikipedia)
- ✅ **The square trees** on the hillside are a homage to **Eyvind Earle**'s angular backgrounds for the 1959 film. Built on a hillside so the architecture reads as an "extension of the building" (slender silhouette). (Disney Parks Blog; Wikipedia) ❔ "Forced perspective" as such not stated in sources fetched.
- ✅ **Stained glass**: **nine arched windows** in La Galerie de la Belle au Bois Dormant (upper floor, opened 20 March 1993) designed/overseen by **Paul Chapman**, English master glazier, former craftsman to the British royal court, who had worked on the restoration of Notre-Dame de Paris's windows and came **out of retirement at 80**. (❌ one fan site calls him "Peter" — correct name Paul.) In 2023 a French craft studio recreated one window. Tapestries and dome paintings also tell the story (maker not identified; ❔ not verified as Aubusson). (Dedicated to DLP; DisneylandParis News)
- ❌ **Dragon length — sources conflict**: **24 m (79 ft), >2 tonnes** per Wikipedia's castle article and Disney Parks Blog ("79-feet long"); 27 m (89 ft), 2,500 kg per Wikipedia's La Tanière article. Safer: "about 24 m — two city buses". It was the **largest Audio-Animatronic ever built when the park opened (1992)**; project led by Imagineer Terri Hardin; it wakes, growls and puffs smoke. Only Disney castle with a dragon underneath.
- ✅ **Mary Blair / It's a Small World**: Blair did the "whimsical design and colour styling" (1964 World's Fair, UNICEF pavilion); the Sherman Brothers' song (July 1963, Cold-War peace message) is per Time "the most publicly performed song of all time". Paris version (12 April 1992): unique façade clock with a wide-awake sun (left) and a sleeping moon (right); first version with a European room, a Middle-Eastern section and a North America scene. (Wikipedia)
- ✅ **Ratatouille land**: exterior recreates Gusteau's restaurant and a Parisian plaza (La Place de Rémy); the ride exits into **Bistrot Chez Rémy**, where diners stay "rat-sized" among oversized props. ❔ Specific props (champagne-cork stools, plate table-tops, cocktail-umbrella parasols) = general knowledge, not re-verified. (Wikipedia)

### A4. Surprising facts for teens (Disneyland)
1. The trees around the castle are **square on purpose** — copied from Eyvind Earle's 1959 backgrounds; and the castle itself quotes a **600-year-old illuminated manuscript** (Très Riches Heures). — https://disneyparksblog.com/disney-experiences/magic-behind-the-castle-hear-from-imagineers-about-the-dreams-for-these-icons/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Ch%C3%A2teau_de_la_Belle_au_Bois_Dormant
2. Because France has **real castles**, Disney built a deliberately *unreal* one — pinker, taller, more Gothic than California's. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Ch%C3%A2teau_de_la_Belle_au_Bois_Dormant
3. The nine windows were made by an **80-year-old** English glazier who had restored **Notre-Dame**'s windows. — https://dedicatedtodlp.com/news/stories-in-stained-glass-illuminating-dlp-details/
4. A **~24 m dragon** sleeps under the castle — the biggest animatronic in the world in 1992. — https://disneyparksblog.com/disney-experiences/magic-behind-the-castle-hear-from-imagineers-about-the-dreams-for-these-icons/
5. "It's a Small World" is officially the **most-performed song in history**, and its look was designed by a woman, Mary Blair, in the 1960s. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_a_Small_World
6. Cascade of Lights uses **swimming drones**, and its theme song was recorded at **Abbey Road** (the Beatles' studio). — https://www.wdwinfo.com/disneyland-paris/new-disney-cascade-of-lights-nighttime-show-uses-aquatic-drones/
7. Right now the castle bridge is hidden behind **walls painted to look like the castle** — a real-life "trompe-l'œil" to hunt for. — https://wdwnt.com/2026/05/sleeping-beauty-castle-bridge-refurbishment-begins-at-disneyland-paris/

### B2. Surprising facts for teens (train)
1. The train enters Marseille through a **7.8 km tunnel** — France's longest all-French rail tunnel — so the sea appears only after you climb out of the station. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGV_M%C3%A9diterran%C3%A9e
2. Saint-Charles' staircase (1925) was built so travellers would **descend into the city like a stage entrance**, past statues of the "faraway lands" Marseille's ships sailed to. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Saint-Charles_station
3. Your La Ciotat station is the one in the **1895/96 Lumière film** — there is a plaque, and 2023 street-art murals in the car park. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ciotat_station

### B3. Looking / making exercise (train)

## C. La Ciotat — Sat 26 – Mon 28 Sept

### C1. Eden Théâtre — Verified / Corrections
- ✅ "L'histoire de l'Eden débute en **1889**" (a Marseille businessman built a café-concert). Billed as "le plus ancien cinéma du monde en activité"; Guinness: oldest purpose-built cinema in operation (rival claim: Ptuj, Slovenia, screening since 1897). Closed 1995 after financial trouble (manager murdered in a 1982 robbery); reopened **9 Oct 2013** after restoration. Also hosted Piaf, Montand, boxing and wrestling. (edencinemalaciotat.com; Wikipedia)
- ❌ Careful with "first public screening 1895": the first paid public screening was **Paris, Salon indien du Grand Café, 28 Dec 1895**. La Ciotat had a **private** screening on **21 Sept 1895** (L'Arroseur arrosé was shown) — the Eden claims it as the venue (❔ location disputed by historians). ❔ The Eden's first *public paid* Lumière screening is usually dated **21 March 1899** — widely stated, not re-verified this session.
- ✅ **Visits**: guided tour **€6, free under 14**, ~1h15–1h30, includes short films on the Eden, the Lumière family and early cinema in La Ciotat; July–Aug: Wed/Fri/Sat 11:00. ❔ **Late-Sept schedule**: the Sept 2026 PDF programme (https://edencinemalaciotat.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/ProgSept2026WEB.pdf) was too large to parse — open it or call **+33 4 88 42 17 60** / contact@edencinemalaciotat.com. Free Heritage-Days tours were 19–20 Sept (before you). "La Décade Malraux" runs **19–27 Sept** → likely evening screenings Sat 26 / Sun 27. Address: 25 bd Georges-Clémenceau (seafront boulevard between old port and Clos des Plages). (edencinemalaciotat.com; agendaculturel)

### C2. The Lumière brothers — Verified / Corrections
- ✅ **L'Arrivée d'un train en gare de La Ciotat**: ~50 s, fixed camera, shot by Louis Lumière at **the same station you arrive at**. Dates are messy: FR sources say filmed summer 1895 and first shown **25 Jan 1896 in Lyon**; EN Wikipedia dates the release 1896 and notes the version everyone sees online is an **1897 reshoot** (women and children boarding). ✅ Certain: it was **NOT** in the 28 Dec 1895 Paris programme. (Wikipedia; Larousse; Le Soleil)
- ✅ **The panic is a myth**: historian **Martin Loiperdinger** ("cinema's founding myth") — no contemporary report of panic; injuries would have made the papers. Maxim Gorky (1896) wrote the train "seems as though it will plunge into the darkness". Tom Gunning: the thrill was like a roller coaster. The "passengers" include **Lumière family members**, and the scene was probably partly staged (nobody looks at the camera). (Wikipedia)
- ✅ **Louis Lumière re-shot the train in stereoscopic 3D** (shown at the Académie des Sciences, 1934/35) — the first 3D remake, by the original director. ❔ The viral 2020 AI-upscaled 4K version (Denis Shiryaev) — general knowledge, not re-verified.
- ✅ **L'Arroseur arrosé** (originally "Le Jardinier et le Petit Espiègle"): filmed in **La Ciotat on the Lumière family property** (fr.wikipedia names "Les Terres rousses" ❔); gardener = **François Clerc**, the family gardener; boy = Léon Trotobas (1st version), then Benoît Duval; shown privately **21 Sept 1895 in La Ciotat**, publicly 28 Dec 1895 in Paris; considered the **first fiction film and first film comedy** (Émile Reynaud's 1892 animated pantomimes are the caveat); subject of the **first film poster** (Marcellin Auzolle, 1896). (fr.wikipedia)
- ✅ **Le Repas de bébé**: Auguste Lumière, his wife Marguerite Winkler and baby Andrée; in the 28 Dec 1895 programme. ❔ Filming location (La Ciotat vs Lyon-Monplaisir) not confirmed — don't claim La Ciotat.
- ✅ **Palais/Château Lumière** (Clos des Plages district, ~200 m from the sea, by Plage Lumière): the family's summer estate where films were made; today **private property divided into apartments/holiday rentals** → exterior only. ❔ No public visit found. (trip.com listings; gralon)
- ✅ **Espace Simon Lumière**, 20 rue du Maréchal Foch (old town): small museum on actor Michel Simon and the Lumières — photos, letters, paintings, old cinema equipment; **€2.30 / €1.15**. ❔ Opening hours — ask the tourist office. (gralon)

### C3. Fauvism 1907 + pétanque — Verified / Corrections
- ✅ **Georges Braque** and **Othon Friesz** (both from Le Havre) painted together in La Ciotat in **summer 1907** (Friesz: late June – late Sept; Braque from ~May, after L'Estaque). Braque's Fauve period lasted under two years (1906–07). (NGA; MuMa Le Havre; Wikipedia)
- ✅ Braque, **"The Port of La Ciotat" (1907)** → **National Gallery of Art, Washington**; NGA text: harbour dominated by the shipyards' large **dry dock**, two steamers in the background. Braque, **"Landscape at La Ciotat" (summer 1907)** → **MoMA, New York**. Other Braque La Ciotat canvases → private/other collections ❔. (nga.gov; moma.org)
- ✅ Friesz painted **at least five versions of the Bec de l'Aigle** in orange, ochre-green and red: **"Paysage (Le Bec de l'Aigle, La Ciotat)" (1907)** → **SFMOMA, San Francisco**; a "La Ciotat" → **National Museum Wales, Cardiff**; others at auction (Sotheby's 2018). MuMa: at La Ciotat "his fauvism reached its apex". ❔ Musée Cantini / Centre Pompidou holdings not verified. ❌ **No local marker, panel or "chemin des peintres" found online** → ❔ ask the tourist office. (sfmoma.org; artuk.org)
- ✅ **The 1907 chain**: Picasso finishes **Les Demoiselles d'Avignon** (Oct 1906 – mid 1907); Braque meets Picasso **"in October or November 1907"**, initially dislikes the painting but studies it closely; the **Cézanne retrospective** (Salon d'Automne 1907) hits the avant-garde; 1908 Braque paints L'Estaque as blocks; critic **Louis Vauxcelles** writes on **14 Nov 1908** that Braque reduces everything to "cubes" (and "bizarreries cubiques" in March 1909) → **Cubism**. So La Ciotat was **Braque's last Fauve summer**. (Wikipedia Braque; Wikipedia Demoiselles) ❔ Vauxcelles also coined "Fauves" in 1905 — general knowledge.
- ✅ **Pétanque was born in La Ciotat**: **1907** — Jules Hugues, dit **"Lenoir"**, a former jeu-provençal champion crippled by rheumatism, draws a circle and plays "pieds tanqués" (Provençal *pèd tanca* = feet planted, no run-up); his friend **Ernest Pitiot** formalised the variant (❔ with brother Joseph at their café "La Boule Étoilée" — secondary sources); **first official tournament 1910** (terrain Béraud near the old port ❔) fixed the name. Today: **Boulodrome Jules Lenoir** on the **Traverse des Pieds Tanqués** — a lane literally called "Planted-Feet Lane"; the association Jules Le Noir keeps the tradition. ❌ **No "Maison de la pétanque" museum found**; ❔ plaque — ask locally. France 2021: **226,502 licensed players**; Thailand is No. 2 (~60,000). (fr.wikipedia; petanque.org; destinationlaciotat.com)

### C4. Nature, port, shipyards — Verified / Corrections
- ✅ **Parc du Mugel**: 12-ha municipal botanical park at the foot of the Bec de l'Aigle; bamboo grove; belvedere at **82 m**; free; **open 1 Apr – 30 Sept, 08:00–20:00** (you are inside summer hours); beaches, picnic areas; Ciotabus line 30 (Mugel/Figuerolles), 15–30 min walk from centre. (Provence tourism; Parc national des Calanques)
- ✅ **Bec de l'Aigle** (155 m): **poudingue** (pudding-stone conglomerate) — rounded pebbles of quartz, sandstone, schist, granite, limestone naturally cemented; deposited ~**90 million years ago (Turonian, Upper Cretaceous)** as a **river delta at the foot of a continent that no longer exists**, to the south (the Provence–Corsica–Sardinia block); the beds dip ~30° to the north, proving the land was south. (The Conversation; myprovence.fr)
- ✅ **Calanque de Figuerolles**: reachable on foot from Mugel/centre (15–30 min). ❔ Rock names ("Le Lion", "Le Capucin") not verified.
- ✅ **Île Verte shuttle "Aquilade I"**: from **12 quai Général-de-Gaulle** (Vieux Port); departures **10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17 h** (13 h & 18 h added some weekends); returns 15 min later; crossing ~10–15 min; **€14 adult / €9 under 10, return**. ❔ Late-Sept weekday operation ("consult us" for October) — call **+33 6 63 59 16 35**. (laciotat-ileverte.com)
- ✅ **La Ciotat Shipyards**: built oil tankers until the 1980s; now a superyacht refit hub (MB92, Monaco Marine). The **grand portique** gantry crane is **93 m high** (75 m under the beam), built **1969** for tanker hull sections, lifts 600 t; the "Atlas" **shiplift takes 4,300 t** — platform for yachts up to 115 m (2022). ❔ Its blue colour — photographic, not textual. (laciotat-shipyards.com)

### C5. Surprising facts for teens (La Ciotat)
1. **Summer 1907, one town, three revolutions**: Braque & Friesz painting the harbour orange; a rheumatic boules champion inventing pétanque a few streets away; and in Paris Picasso finishing Les Demoiselles d'Avignon — which Braque sees weeks later, dropping Fauvism for "cubes". — https://www.nga.gov/collection/art-object-page.106383.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Braque ; https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9tanque
2. Braque's La Ciotat harbour hangs in **Washington**, Friesz's Bec de l'Aigle in **San Francisco** and **Cardiff** — the view is still here, the paintings went round the world. — https://www.sfmoma.org/artwork/81.52/ ; https://artuk.org/discover/artworks/la-ciotat-160182
3. The famous train film was **not** the first film shown, nobody **panicked**, the family were the **extras**, and Louis Lumière later **re-shot it in 3D**. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Arriv%C3%A9e_d'un_train_en_gare_de_La_Ciotat
4. The world's **first comedy / first fiction film** was shot in a La Ciotat garden with the family's real gardener as the victim — and became the first film poster. — https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%27Arroseur_arros%C3%A9
5. La Ciotat has a lane called **Traverse des Pieds Tanqués** — "Planted-Feet Lane" — where the game with 226,000 licensed French players began. — https://en.destinationlaciotat.com/explore/the-cultural-heritage/petanque/
6. The Bec de l'Aigle is made of **pebbles from a vanished continent**, dumped by a river 90 million years ago and tilted 30°. — https://theconversation.com/le-bec-de-laigle-temoin-dun-continent-disparu-entre-corse-et-provence-162692
7. The 93 m crane built in 1969 to assemble **supertankers** now lifts **billionaires' yachts** — and it's in the background of Braque's painting as a dry dock. — https://www.laciotat-shipyards.com/en/equipment-and-services/gantry-crane/

### C6. Looking / making exercises (La Ciotat)
- **Station (arrival, Fri)**: stand on the platform where Louis Lumière put his camera (diagonal composition, train coming toward you); film the TER arriving for 50 s, phone still. Compare with the 1895/97 frame. Find the plaque and the 2023 murals in the car park.
- **Fauve harbour (Sat)**: at the Vieux Port facing the shipyards, each girl chooses three "wrong" colours (orange rock, green sea, pink sky — like Friesz) and makes a 10-minute colour sketch; then compare with Braque's NGA painting on a phone — spot the dry dock/crane then and now.
- **Pétanque (Sat/Sun)**: at Boulodrome Jules Lenoir, draw the circle, feet planted, one round each; the "looking" part: watch how a player's body freezes like a photograph while the ball moves like film.
- **Bec de l'Aigle / Mugel (Sun/Mon)**: "read the rock" — find five differently coloured pebbles in the poudingue (each came from a different mountain on a continent that no longer exists) and sketch the 30° tilt of the layers.

- ✅ **Cap Canaille / Falaises Soubeyranes: highest sea cliffs in France, max 394 m** (on the La Ciotat side). **Route des Crêtes (D141)** follows the cliff edge between Cassis and La Ciotat with viewpoints. (fr.wikipedia)
- ❌ **Route des Crêtes is closed to motor vehicles EVERY SUNDAY, all year** (walkers/cyclists only; shuttle from the Gorguettes car park on the Cassis side in season) — so **Sunday 27 Sept = no car/taxi on the Crêtes**; also closed on **red fire-risk days** (levels published from 18:00 the day before on myprovence.fr / "My Calanques" app). (destinationlaciotat.com; Parc national des Calanques)

- ✅ **Art in Cassis**: **Paul Signac** painted five neo-impressionist canvases here in **1889** ("Cassis, Cap Lombard, Opus 196"; "Cap Canaille, Cassis"); **André Derain** spent several weeks in Cassis in **summer 1907** ("Landscape near Cassis") — i.e. Derain in Cassis while Braque & Friesz were in La Ciotat: the whole coast was a Fauve studio in 1907. (Wikipedia; secondary sources)

### D2. Surprising facts for teens (Cassis)
1. In summer 1907 the Fauves were **8 km apart**: Derain in Cassis, Braque & Friesz in La Ciotat, all painting the same red cliff from opposite sides. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassis ; https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79554
2. **Cap Canaille (394 m)** is orange-red conglomerate/sandstone, while the calanques next door are **white limestone** — you can see the colour border from the boat. — https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_Canaille_(Cassis)
3. Signac painted Cassis in **dots** (1889); Derain 18 years later in **flat slabs of colour** — same light, two grammars. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassis,_Cap_Lombard,_Opus_196

### D3. Looking / making exercise (Cassis)
On the boat: "two grammars" — each girl draws the same cliff twice in 3 minutes each: once in dots (Signac), once in flat colour patches (Derain). Then find the exact line where orange rock meets white rock.
