## 1. St Pancras / King's Cross
### Verified / Corrections
- Opened 1868 ✅*; Barlow's train-shed roof 73 m span — largest single-span roof in the world when built ✅*.
- Betjeman led the 1966–67 campaign that got the station Grade I listed (Nov 1967) ✅*; statue by Martin Jennings (2007) ✅*.
- *The Meeting Place* by Paul Day, 9 m bronze (2007) ✅*.
- Public pianos ✅* (one donated by Elton John, 2016).
- Platform 9¾ trolley photo-op with queue at King's Cross ✅*.
### Surprising facts for teens
- The undercroft's column grid was set by the size of Burton beer barrels — the basement was designed as a beer warehouse (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Pancras_railway_station) ✅*.
- The roof's sky-blue is the original 1868 "Barlow blue", found under later paint during the 2007 restoration ✅*.
- The frieze around *The Meeting Place* (added 2008) sparked a row: one panel showed a commuter falling in front of a Tube train driven by a skull-faced driver — it was removed ✅*.
- The clock is a replica: the original was dropped and smashed in the 1970s while being sold to an American buyer; a railwayman bought the shards for £25 and rebuilt it in his barn ✅*.
### Looking exercise
Stand under the arch and draw the roof ribs as one continuous line without lifting the pen. Then compare scale: sketch Betjeman (life-size, looking up) and the 9 m couple on the same page — which one is the "monument"?

## 2. West End theatres
### Verified / Corrections
- ~40 West End theatres ✅* (SOLT counts 39–40).
- Gillian Lynne Theatre: opened Jan 1973 as the New London; *Cats* 11 May 1981 – 11 May 2002, 8,949 performances; renamed 2018 ✅*.
- *My Neighbour Totoro*: playing there, booking to 3 Jan 2027 ✅; running time **2 h 30 incl. interval ❌ (book says 2 h 45)**; age 6+, under-4s not admitted ✅.
- Cambridge Theatre 1930 ✅*; *Matilda* since 2011 ✅*; 7 Oliviers 2012 ✅*; running time 2 h 35; Sunday performance 15:00 (finishes ~17:35) ✅.
- Victoria Palace 1911 ✅*; Mackintosh refurbishment 2014–17 ✅*; *Hamilton* since Dec 2017 ✅*; Mon–Sat 19:30, Thu & Sat 14:30, 2 h 45 ✅; booking to 13 Mar 2027 ✅. Monday 19:30 exists ✅.
- Ghost light tradition ✅* (folklore); TodayTix Rush/day seats ✅*.
### Surprising facts for teens
- The New London's stalls revolved: at the start of *Cats* part of the audience turned 180° with the set (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Lynne_Theatre) ✅*.
- Gillian Lynne was the first non-royal woman to have a West End theatre named after her (2018) ✅*.
- Victoria Palace's cupola carried a gilded statue of ballerina Anna Pavlova until 1939; it vanished in WWII and a replica went back up in 2006 ✅*.
- Totoro's puppets were built with Jim Henson's Creature Shop and the production won 6 Oliviers ✅.
### Looking exercise
Before curtain-up, photograph the proscenium and find every hidden letter/word in Rob Howell's *Matilda* set (alphabet-block frame). After the show: name one moment where the lighting alone told the story.

## 3. Fitzrovia
### Verified / Corrections

- Sold to MCR Hotels Feb 2024 (£275m) ✅; **status Sept 2026: still closed. Planning consent expected Sept 2026, construction from 2029, hotel + public floors 31–35 opening 2033** ✅.
- **George Orwell lived at 18 Percy St ❌** — that was Sonia Brownell's flat (Orwell's second wife); Orwell visited. Orwell drank at the Fitzroy Tavern and the Wheatsheaf ✅*.
- Fitzroy Tavern: Orwell, Dylan Thomas ✅*.
- Broadcasting House 1932; Eric Gill's *Prospero and Ariel* ✅*. Statue was attacked in 2022 and 2023 over Gill's diaries; current restoration/plaque state ❔.
- **Pollock's Toy Museum ❌ closed at 1 Scala St in Jan 2023**; pop-ups Whitgift Centre Croydon (Sat) and Leadenhall Market (Wed/Fri) reported 2024; no permanent home announced; 2026 status ❔.

### Surprising facts for teens
- Eric Gill, sculptor of *Prospero and Ariel*, also designed the Gill Sans typeface (1928) — the font of British Railways and Penguin books. His teacher was Edward Johnston, who designed the Underground's font (1916) and the roundel's proportions (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gill_Sans) ✅*.
- 29 Fitzroy Square has one plaque for two residents: George Bernard Shaw (1887–98) and Virginia Woolf (1907–11) ✅*.
- The BT Tower was Britain's tallest building until 1980 (NatWest Tower) ✅*.
### Looking exercise
"Framing": from Mortimer St find three gaps between buildings that frame the BT Tower; photograph each with the same zoom. Then spot Gill Sans in the wild (BBC signage) versus Johnston on a Tube sign — what differs in the letter "a" and "g"?

## 4. Postal Museum + Mail Rail
### Verified / Corrections
- Mail Rail opened 3 Dec 1927, closed 31 May 2003 ✅; driverless ✅; ~4 million items/day at peak ✅; opened to visitors 5 Sept 2017 ✅; ride ~15 min ✅.
- Hours Tue–Sun 10:00–17:00, last ride 16:00; ticket = annual pass, ride valid on first visit ✅.
- Penny Black 1840 ✅*; UK the only country not naming itself on stamps ✅*.
- **Sorted! play space: ages 1–8, 45-min timed sessions, separate ticket** ✅ (perfect for the 3-year-old).
### Surprising facts for teens
- Trains hit 40 mph in the tunnels, 21 m below the street; the line was inspired by Chicago's freight tunnels and closed because it cost 5× more than road vans (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Post_Office_Railway) ✅.
- The Penny Black portrait is Queen Victoria aged 15, copied from William Wyon's 1837 City Medal — she kept that teenage image on stamps for 60 years ✅*.
- The Penny Black lasted one year: its red cancellation could be wiped off, so the Penny Red replaced it in 1841 ✅*.
### Looking exercise
Design a one-colour stamp: draw each sibling's profile in silhouette (as Wyon did), then decide which single colour and which corner letters go on it.

## 7. Whitehall / Westminster
### Verified / Corrections
- Big Ben bell 13.7 t ✅*; cracked 1859 ✅*; Elizabeth Tower 2012 ✅*.
- Pre-decimal pennies on the pendulum: each speeds the clock 0.4 s/day ✅ (parliament.uk).
- Westminster Bridge green (Commons), Lambeth Bridge red (Lords) ✅*.
- Monarch barred from the Commons since 1642 ✅*.
- **Sunset Sun 20 Sept 2026: 19:02 BST** (19 Sept 19:04, 21 Sept 19:00, 22 Sept 18:57) ✅.
- LDF 2026 = 12–20 Sept ✅. **South Bank installations ❌**: no Landmark Project there; nearby LDF pieces to 20 Sept: *Pangolin Shield* (St Mary le Strand, Aldwych), Mykola Kabluka's *Soul* / *The Point of Unity* (St Paul's Churchyard, 3,500 fibre-optic points lit after dark), Oxo Tower Wharf open studios (Bankside, 9–20 Sept) ✅.
### Surprising facts for teens
- Turner's *Fighting Temeraire* aside — the tower itself leans: a 2011 survey found it tilting ~0.26° north (about 46 cm at the top) (https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/big-ben/facts-figures/) ✅*.
- The bell still has its 1859 crack: they turned it an eighth of a turn and fitted a lighter hammer — the crack is why its note sounds "off" ✅*.
- The tower was Augustus Pugin's last design; he said "I never worked so hard in my life" before his breakdown ✅*.
### Looking exercise
Colour-match the bridges: photograph Westminster green and Lambeth red side by side, then find those exact colours inside the Commons/Lords benches on a postcard. Bonus: hold a phone spirit-level app against the tower's edge.

## 14. National Gallery
### Verified / Corrections
- **Sunflowers ❌ NOT ON DISPLAY — on loan to Philadelphia (6 June–11 Oct 2026)** ✅. Painted Arles, Aug 1888, for Gauguin's room ✅ (NG).
- Rooms (NG website, 15 Sept 2026): **Turner *Fighting Temeraire* Room 40 ✅; Holbein *Ambassadors* Room 4 ✅; Seurat *Bathers at Asnières* Room 44 ✅ (Art Newspaper May 2025 said 45 — read the door numbers); Van Gogh *A Wheatfield with Cypresses* Room 43 ✅**. *Van Gogh's Chair* page not found today ❔.
- Hours daily 10:00–18:00, Fri to 21:00; Monday open ✅; entrance via Sainsbury Wing (west side, step-free) ✅; free, optional timed ticket = fast track ✅.
- 20 min at 16:45: feasible — Rooms 40–44 are at the far east end (~6–8 min walk from Sainsbury entrance); rooms start clearing ~17:50 ✅*.
### Surprising facts for teens
- Van Gogh signed "Vincent" because nobody could pronounce his surname; one of the seven Sunflowers was destroyed in WWII (https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/vincent-van-gogh-sunflowers) ✅.
- Turner painted the sunset in the east — impossible for a ship towed up the Thames; he also moved the tug's funnel to make more smoke. He called it "my Darling" and refused to sell (https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/joseph-mallord-william-turner-the-fighting-temeraire) ✅.
- Holbein's skull only snaps into shape from the bottom-right corner; a lute string is broken and a hymn book lies open — a coded picture of Europe splitting (https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/hans-holbein-the-younger-the-ambassadors) ✅.
- Seurat was 24, the Salon rejected *Bathers*; he later went back and added dots to the orange hat — you can see the technique switch mid-painting (https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/georges-seurat-bathers-at-asnieres) ✅.
- *Wheatfield with Cypresses* was finished inside his hospital room at Saint-Rémy; he wanted to paint cypresses "like the sunflowers" ✅.
### Looking exercise
Anamorphosis: crouch at the right edge of *The Ambassadors* until the skull forms. Back at the hotel, write a word stretched so it reads only from one angle. Second: in Room 44 find the exact spot where Seurat's smooth paint turns into dots.

### Surprising facts for teens

- *Umbra Sumus* ("we are shadows") is from Horace — a Roman poet quoted on a Huguenot church that became a synagogue and then a mosque ✅*.
